Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam

"The Missile Man of India" — Scientist, Educator, and 11th President of India

About Dr. Kalam

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was an Indian aerospace scientist who served as the 11th President of India from 2002 to 2007. Born and raised in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, he studied physics and aerospace engineering. He spent the next four decades as a scientist and science administrator, mainly at the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) and Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO).

He was intimately involved in India's civilian space program and military missile development efforts. He thus came to be known as the Missile Man of India for his work on the development of ballistic launch vehicle and missile technology.

"Dream, dream, dream. Dreams transform into thoughts and thoughts result in action."

— Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam

Key Achievements & Milestones

  • 1969: Transferred to ISRO, where he was the project director for India's first Indigenous Satellite Launch Vehicle (SLV-III).
  • 1980s: Directed the Integrated Guided Missile Development Program (IGMDP), leading to successful projects like Agni and Prithvi.
  • 1998: Played a pivotal organizational and technical role in India's Pokhran-II nuclear tests.
  • 2002–2007: Served as the 11th President of India, widely beloved as the "People's President."
  • 2015: Honored globally for his dedication to teaching, writing, and inspiring millions of youth worldwide.
